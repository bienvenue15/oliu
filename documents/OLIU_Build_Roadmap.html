Corridor MIS · build road · one feature at a time

Twelve slices. Each one used by real people before the next is started.

The system in the analysis is large. The way to build it without drowning a developer is to treat it like the corridor itself: one station at a time, a test at each gate, and the container does not move on until the gate is passed. Every slice below says what gets built, which menus of the mockup it switches on, the runs the developer walks through with real OLIU data, the test that must pass, and what the Managing Director sees when it does. The owner portal, for you, comes in two stages at the end, when there is a second and a third account to justify it.

Rule of the road: a slice is finished when staff have used it on live bookings for a week, the test passed, the security checklist ran, and its slice note is written. Not when the code is written.

Spine2 weeks

Everything that comes later is scoped by two questions: which account, which company. Build that first, with nothing inside it, and the rest of the system can never leak across companies by accident.

The owner portal at this stage is one form you use yourself to create OLIU · JIVAH. No tenant list, no billing. It is the same set-up screen the tenant later sees under Settings.

Runs the developer walks through

  1. Create the account. Answer the set-up questions: two companies, OLIU bills the client, hand-over at Rusumo. Nothing else is asked yet.
  2. Invite twelve people. Each gets one role and one company; the MD gets all. OTP arrives at finance@, ops@, management@.
  3. Sign in as each person. The sidebar shows only the empty menus their role may open. A JIVAH login never sees the OLIU company in the switcher.
  4. Suspend one user. Their next sign-in is refused and the attempt is in the audit trail.
Pass whenTwelve logins, each scoped correctly, and the audit trail shows every sign-in. A single-company test account shows no switcher at all.MD seesTheir own login on the phone, the company switcher, and a list of who has access. Nothing else exists yet, and that is correct.

Not built yet: no bookings, no containers, no money. Resist adding a screen "while we are here".

Payments: request, approve, pay, lock

Module3 weeks

Every payment is a fixed state. Nothing is paid without one-click approval, a paid order locks, and the same box plus category plus beneficiary is refused a second time and sent to the MD instead.

What gets built

Beneficiary with protected account (four-eyes on change)Payment request from a cost line or a tipApprove with MFA above thresholdPay and lockDuplicate block → MD queueFloats and settlementOffice expenses through the same queue
Menus switched on in the tenant portal
Payments → Needs payment: approval queuePayments → Pay — Finance OfficerPayments → BeneficiariesChecks → MD queue: duplicate container, repeat paymentSettings → Configuration: approval thresholds, MFA

Runs

  1. Request payment for a clearing fee. Operations Manager approves with one click; above the threshold the code arrives at the department mailbox.
  2. Pay it. The order locks. It cannot be edited by anyone, including the MD.
  3. Request the same box, fee and payee again. It is blocked and lands in the MD queue with both orders attached.
  4. Change a beneficiary's bank account. It waits for a second person before any payment can use it.
  5. Settle a float. The cash-in-hands figure drops the moment the settlement is approved.
Pass whenA month of real payments ran through the queue, every paid order is locked, one duplicate was caught before the bank, and no payment exists without an approval.MD seesApprovals waiting on the phone, cash out this month, and the first blocked duplicate.

Blind Finance entry and the compare job

Dar side openModule3 weeks

The most important control in the system, built only now because both trails must exist first. Finance enters category, beneficiary and amount from the paper in hand, never seeing Operations' figure. A background job compares the two trails and flags a disagreement to the MD. A match is silent.

What gets built

Needs-payment queue: container only, no category, no amountFinance record (category, beneficiary, amount, from paper)Compare job every 15 minFlag with both figures, both authors, both timesMD ruling as third recordTolerance as configuration"Single trail" marker for a side without blind entry
Menus switched on in the tenant portal
Payments → Pay — Finance Officer: blind viewChecks → ReconciliationChecks → Audit trailSettings → Configuration: match tolerance
Dar side: lines carry a single-trail marker and show amber on the Reconciliation tab until the MD decides.

Runs, Kigali pattern

  1. Operations enters RWF 1,900,000 for leg 2. The Finance Officer's queue shows only "PCIU 9084297 has a pending item".
  2. Finance enters RWF 1,909,000 from invoice 5541. She never saw the first figure. The compare job pairs the two lines.
  3. The flag reaches the MD with both figures. He rules Finance is right; the ruling is stored, neither record is overwritten.
  4. Enter a matching pair. Nothing happens. Silence is the healthy state.
Dar sideOne person in Dar records both operations and finance today, so the Kigali pattern would report a match by construction. Left open until the MD decides between: Kigali Finance entering Dar's trail from photographed paper; the MD as Dar's second trail; big Dar costs paid by Kigali payment order with only field cash single-trail; or Dar marked single-trail and shown as such. The slice ships for Kigali either way; Dar lines carry the single-trail marker until the decision.
Pass whenA deliberate mismatch and a real one were both flagged to the MD within fifteen minutes, matches produced no noise for a week, and the Finance Officer confirms she never saw Operations' figure.MD seesThe reconciliation flags, both trails side by side, and the first ruling he made as a third record.

About six months for one developer to a system OLIU runs its month on, then six more weeks for the owner portal, timed to the second and third accounts rather than to the calendar. Three developers in parallel can shorten the tenant slices only after slice 3, because slices 1 to 3 are the spine everything else hangs on; a realistic parallel plan is slices 4, 5, 6 together, then 7, 8, 9 together, which lands OLIU in late January. Each week-count includes the week of live use that closes the slice. The proposal's 14 December date is reachable only by shipping slices 1, 2, 3, 6 and 8 first and the rest from February; that is a decision for the MD, not a default.

How the code will be written, promised before slice 1

These are commitments, not wishes. Each one is checked at the gate of every slice, together with the test.

  • Built to resist attack. No system is unhackable, so the promise is specific: every request checked against the signed-in user's account, company and role on the server, never trusted from the browser; passwords hashed, one-time codes to the department mailbox, sessions that expire; every input validated and every database query parameterised; files scanned and stored outside the web root; rate limits on login and OTP; secrets in the environment, never in the code; dependencies pinned and checked for known holes at every slice; an attack checklist (OWASP top ten) run before a slice is called done.
  • Clean. One language front and back, one style enforced by a formatter and a linter, small functions with one job, names that say what a thing is in the business's own words: container, cost_entry, payment_order. No cleverness that needs a comment to explain it.
  • Efficient. Every list is paged and indexed by account and company; the compare job, digest and dunning run in the background, never while a user waits; the MD's phone pages load under a second on a Kigali connection; money arithmetic is integer, so it is both exact and fast.
  • Easy to understand. The folder tree mirrors the modules on this page, so a reader finds Payments under payments. Each module has a short README saying what it owns and what events it sends. Tests read like the runs on this page, with real OLIU numbers.
  • A slice note after every slice. Before the next slice starts, a plain-language document for you: what was built, which tables and screens, how a request travels through the code, what was tested and how, what was deliberately left out, and the questions for you. Short, with screenshots, kept with the code. Those notes, read in order, become the handover manual for any future developer.

Why this order and not the proposal's

  • Spine first. Account, container, cost. Without those three nothing else can be tested on real data.
  • Value early. After slice 3, six weeks in, OLIU already knows what each box cost. That is the first thing the owner asked for.
  • The control last among the core. Blind entry and compare need both trails to exist, so it sits at slice 7, not slice 1 where its importance would tempt you to put it.
  • Invoices after payments. The client invoice waits until the box's own costs are covered, so payments must exist before the issue gate can be real.
  • Import late. Migrating history into a system that is still changing shape is wasted work. Migrate once, into a system that has held a live month.
  • Owner portal last, in two stages. With one tenant it is a form you fill yourself. It becomes a screen when the second account is onboarded and a billing system when the third signs. Building it earlier would be building for customers who do not exist.

What stays open while we build

  • Dar blind entry. Slice 7 ships for Kigali. Dar lines carry a single-trail marker until the MD chooses one of the four arrangements. No code depends on the choice.
  • Who bills the client: decided, OLIU. The August analysis said JIVAH; the MD has confirmed OLIU, as the October invoices show. Set at account creation in slice 1; the invoice slice reads it.
  • Reconciliation tolerance. Exact match by default, a band for conversion drift. A setting in slice 7.
  • Escort with the cargo, or dispatcher. Decides how hard slice 5's offline mode has to work. Build offline anyway; it costs little.
