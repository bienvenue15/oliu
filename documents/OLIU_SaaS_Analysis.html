2 · Why today's way leaks

Two companies, one shared Excel file, WhatsApp and phone calls. Nobody can say what a box cost until weeks after it sailed. The proposal's issues paper lists thirteen problems; they come down to four.

LeakHow it happens todayWhat stops it
The same invoice paid twiceA supplier is paid from a message; nobody checks it was paid already. Duplicates surface only if the supplier says so.Same beneficiary, box and amount is blocked at request and sent to the MD queue. A paid order locks.
Costs billed late or neverInvoices are typed from the sheet after the box has left; numbering has gaps; a cost found later is written off.The invoice is built from the container records at departure. Gapless numbers. A box closes only when its costs are in.
Cash in staff handsFacilitation payments reported from memory or photos, no ceiling, reconciled weeks later, payee unknown.Three-tap entry with payee type and MoMo ID, per-tip ceiling, monthly cap with alerts at 80 % and 100 %.
Nobody is accountableOne shared login, cells overwritten, no history of who changed what.Individual logins, six roles, append-only audit trail, four-eyes on bank details.

3 · The four controls that make the numbers trustworthy

Control 1

Two trails, compared by a job

Operations records what a box cost from the field. Finance records it again from the paper invoice in hand. A job compares them every 15 minutes. A mismatch becomes a flag; the MD's ruling is the third record.

Control 2

Blind entry

The Finance Officer never sees Operations' figure. Slower in the moment, and the whole system rests on it. The open question is Dar es Salaam, where one person may hold both trails today.

Control 3

Nothing resolves locally

Duplicate container, repeat payment, replacement leg: each goes to the MD queue with both records attached. Month-end becomes a review of flags already ruled on, about one day instead of five.

Control 4

The invoice is built, not typed

Every column on the real OLIU invoice already exists in the container record: departure date, vessel, ETD, BL №, container №, size, lot №, rate. The system composes it; a person reviews and issues.

5 · From one client to a product: two packages

The container record itself never splits. It carries a responsible company that flips when the hand-over stage is confirmed, and that one flip moves the box between working lists, decides which book a cost defaults into, and produces the inter-company picture the owner sees in Group overview.

Why this keeps the product flexible

  • Company count is a setting, not a product line. A single-company tenant that opens a Tanzanian company moves to the group package by adding a company. Nothing is migrated. Pricing is left out of this analysis for now.
  • The six roles are the same in both packages. A role is attached to one company, or to all for the owner. That is the only scoping rule, and it is set when a user is invited.
  • Per company you decide four things: country and currency, bank accounts that print on invoices, who bills the client, and where the hand-over point is.
  • One screen, two behaviours. The Shipments board, Payments queue and Invoices list filter by the signed-in user's company. The owner's switcher is the only element a single-company tenant never sees.

Two portals

Tenant portal · the forwarder

All 29 screens of the original MIS, grouped under ten menus: Home, Shipments, Field payments, Payments, Invoices, Accounting, Reports, Checks, Notifications, Settings. The account carries the company and the role; there is nothing to pick after login.

Platform portal · you

Tenants as accounts, plans, billing with automatic reminders, onboarding progress, support tickets, health, releases. It sees counts, never contents. Looking inside a tenant needs its MD's approval and expires after four hours. That rule is the strongest sales argument to an owner-manager.

7 · Every feature stands alone

Section 09 of the systems analysis set the rule: a modular monolith, built so that removing a feature breaks nothing. Each feature owns its tables and its screens and talks to the others only through a small, named interface, an event such as container.closed or a lookup such as "cost entries for container X". No feature ever reads another feature's tables directly. The product keeps that rule and adds one more: a feature can be switched off per account, and switching it off hides screens, never deletes records.

The test, in three questions

  • Remove the folder. Does its sidebar entry disappear and everything else keep running? If another feature stops, the boundary was wrong.
  • Read the code. Does the feature reach into another feature's tables? Then it must go through that feature's interface instead.
  • Switch it off for one account. Do the records it already created stay readable in reports and the audit trail? They must.

How the code will be written

  • Built to resist attack. Every request is checked on the server against the user's account, company and role; inputs validated, queries parameterised, secrets outside the code, dependencies pinned and checked, an OWASP top-ten review at every slice. No system is unhackable; this is the discipline that makes an attack expensive and visible.
  • Clean and easy to understand. One language, one enforced style, small functions named in the business's own words, a folder per module with a short README. A future developer in Kigali should be able to read it without the author.
  • Efficient. Paged and indexed lists, background jobs for anything slow, integer money arithmetic, fast pages on a phone connection.
  • Documented after every slice. A plain-language slice note: what was built, how a request travels through the code, what was tested, what was left out, and the open questions. Read in order, the notes are the handover manual.

Room to grow

  • A new feature is a new folder that subscribes to existing events. Fiscal receipts, a driver app, dispatch planning or the AI items from section 08 of the analysis would each arrive this way, without touching what exists.
  • A new country is settings: currency, document checklist, stage names, checkpoint types. Nothing in the code knows the word Rwanda.
  • A new corridor is a template of stages the owner picks or edits at set-up: Kigali to Dar export today, Kigali to Mombasa or an import route tomorrow.
  • A new company in an account is a row, with its own book, bank accounts and staff, placed on the journey where it takes over.

9 · What is deliberately left out

  • No tax-authority integration. No EBM, no VFD, no eTIMS. Invoices are numbered PDFs emailed from the system. A fiscal receipt, where a company issues one, is a document filed by hand. This can come later as its own module if a tenant asks and the certification is worth it.
  • No shipping-line, port or bank APIs. Everything is entered by a person; the system's job is to stop the same number being typed with two meanings.
  • No driver app, dispatch planning or CRM. The field screen is for the person who pays at a roadblock, nothing more.
  • Accounting is a module switched on later. Ledger, bank matching, payroll and IFRS statements come as a module from any month-start. Until then the accountant gets clean Excel exports.

11 · Feasibility scorecard

Technical
strong
Ordinary web application, manual entry, no integrations. Tenant isolation and the compare job are well-understood work. Three developers for fourteen weeks is tight but was already planned.
Need
strong
The leaks are real and priced; the two October invoices show the manual work. Owners of small forwarders feel every one of the four leaks.
Market
unproven
Freight forwarders on the Central Corridor number in the low hundreds across Rwanda, Tanzania, Uganda and Burundi. Enough for a profitable niche, not for growth capital. Count them.
Team
thin
After go-live the platform needs one person on support and one on the product, plus someone who sells to owners in Kinyarwanda and Swahili. Today that is mostly you.
Flexibility
strong
Two packages, one codebase. Company count, hand-over point and per-company visibility are answers given at set-up, and every feature can be switched off for an account without touching the others. A client can start single and grow into a group without a migration.
Timing
good
OLIU live 14 December, JIVAH 21 December. Second tenant from February. Product decisions can wait until the pilot shows what owners actually ask for.

Verdict: feasible, as a focused niche product, provided two things happen.

First, build OLIU's system on a tenant-aware foundation now, with the company scoping from section 06 of the systems analysis built in from day one, so the second customer is configuration rather than a rewrite and both packages ship from the same code. Second, keep the platform's intellectual property when the contract is signed. Everything else, from the second region to the accounting module, can follow the customers.
